3 AI Tools to Reclaim
5 Hours of Your Week

5 min read AI Productivity UK May 2025 @coetz.ai

Most people use AI wrong. They open ChatGPT, type a vague question, get a mediocre answer, and decide "AI isn't for me." This guide isn't about AI in the abstract — it's about three specific tools doing three specific jobs that used to eat half my working day. Combined, they return at least five hours every week.

Before we start

This isn't a sponsored post. No affiliate commissions are steering these recommendations. I use all three of these tools daily in my own workflow. Free tiers are available for all of them.

The Time Problem

I tracked my working hours for two weeks before adopting these tools. The breakdown was embarrassing. Around 90 minutes a day went on email — reading, drafting, triaging. Another hour on research: opening fifteen tabs, skimming, taking notes, losing the tab. And roughly 45 minutes on "starting" — the blank page problem before any piece of writing.

That's nearly four hours, every day, on tasks that are largely mechanical. Here's what replaced them.

01
Perplexity AI — Research in minutes, not hours

Perplexity is a search engine built on AI that returns cited, synthesised answers instead of a list of links to wade through. Ask it "What are the best AI scheduling tools for small businesses in the UK?" and you get a structured answer with sources, not ten blue links.

How I use it: Every time I need to research a tool, topic, or market before writing, I start with Perplexity. I'll ask a broad question, then drill down with follow-ups. A research task that used to take 45 minutes — opening tabs, reading, losing the thread — now takes 10–15 minutes.

Best prompt structure: "Give me a comprehensive overview of [topic], focusing on [specific angle]. Include recent developments from 2024–2025 and cite your sources."

Free tier available Pro: ~£17/month Time saved: ~40 min/day
02
Claude by Anthropic — Your first draft engine

Claude is the AI I use for all long-form writing: email drafts, newsletter sections, Instagram captions, article outlines. It follows instructions more precisely than any other model I've tested, and it doesn't pad its responses with filler.

How I use it: I give Claude the R-C-F framework — Role, Context, Format — and it produces first drafts I can edit rather than write from scratch. An email that used to take 20 minutes of staring takes three minutes: one minute to write the prompt, two minutes to edit the output.

The inbox workflow: I paste incoming emails I need to respond to into Claude with the instruction: "Draft a reply that is direct, friendly, and under 150 words. I want to [outcome]. Don't start with 'I hope this email finds you well.'" Then I edit the result.

Free tier available Pro: ~£15/month Time saved: ~1.5 hrs/day
03
Make.com — Automate the truly repetitive stuff

Make (formerly Integromat) is a no-code automation platform. Think of it as the connective tissue between your apps. It runs automations in the background so you never have to do the same manual task twice.

How I use it: Three automations that run daily without me touching them: (1) New Instagram post performance is logged automatically into a Notion database every morning. (2) New email newsletter subscribers from the website get added to my email list and tagged. (3) Any article I save to Pocket with the tag "blog-idea" gets added to a Notion content calendar row.

Learning curve note: Make has a steeper learning curve than Zapier. Give it two evenings. The free tier covers 1,000 operations/month, which is enough for most solo workflows.

Free tier: 1,000 ops/month Core: ~£8/month Time saved: ~45 min/day

The Honest Time Breakdown

Here's what five hours back per week actually looks like in practice:

Task Before After Saved

Research 60 min/day 15 min/day 45 min/day
Manual data tasks 45 min/day 0 min/day 45 min/day
Total (5-day week) 14.5 hrs ~7 hrs ~7.5 hrs
Reality check

Your savings will vary. If email isn't your time sink, the gains there won't apply. Track your own time for a week first — the results are usually more alarming than expected. Focus on your biggest drain, not mine.

How to Get Started This Week

Don't try to implement all three at once. Pick the one that maps to your biggest time drain and spend one day getting it working properly.

  1. Biggest drain: research? Sign up for Perplexity free and use it for every research task this week. Nothing else.
  2. Biggest drain: writing/email? Get Claude free, bookmark it, and use the R-C-F prompt structure for every draft you write.
  3. Biggest drain: repetitive manual tasks? List the three things you do manually more than twice a week. Build one Make automation for the most painful one.

Once any of these becomes habit — usually two weeks — add the next tool. Stacking them is where the hours really compound.
