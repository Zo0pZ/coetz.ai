Make.com vs Zapier:
Which Is Worth Paying For?

6 min read Automation May 2025 UK pricing

Both tools promise to automate everything. Both have free tiers. Both have enough features to confuse you. The difference is where they earn their place — and for most UK solo operators and small teams, one is significantly better value than the other.

Test setup

Three real automations, built on both platforms using their respective free tiers and then paid plans. Judged on: ease of setup, reliability, feature depth, and cost efficiency. No affiliate commission from either platform.

The pricing model difference matters more than the headline numbers. Make charges per operation (each step in a workflow). Zapier charges per task (each time a Zap runs, regardless of steps). A 5-step Make scenario costs 5 operations per run; a 5-step Zapier Zap costs 1 task per run. For complex multi-step workflows, Zapier is more efficient on paper — but Make's absolute price ceiling is lower for most solo/small team use cases.

Test 1: Email Parsing → CRM Entry

Automation: when a new email matching a specific subject pattern lands in Gmail, extract the sender name and company from the email body, then create a new contact record in a Notion database.

Make.com vs Zapier
Email parsing → Notion CRM entry
Make result Handled email body parsing with built-in regex tools. The visual scenario builder made it easy to map the extracted text to the right Notion properties. Setup: ~35 minutes first time. Reliable on all test runs.
Zapier result Easier initial setup with better guided UI, but email body parsing required a workaround (Email Parser by Zapier — a separate tool that adds steps). Took ~25 minutes but the parser setup adds complexity. Less flexible.
Winner Make.com ✓

Test 2: Instagram Post → Analytics Log

Automation: 24 hours after a new Instagram post, pull the post's engagement metrics and log them as a row in a Google Sheet.

Make.com vs Zapier
Instagram analytics → Google Sheets log
Make result Native Instagram module available. The 24-hour delay required a Sleep module, which worked correctly. Setup: ~20 minutes. Ran reliably across the two-week test period.
Zapier result Also has a native Instagram integration. The delay was simpler to configure via the built-in "Delay" step. Better documentation for this specific use case. Slightly faster to set up (~15 mins). Equally reliable.
Winner Zapier ✓

Test 3: Invoice Reminder Sequence

Automation: when an invoice in FreshBooks passes its due date unpaid, send a polite reminder email via Gmail after 3 days, then a firmer follow-up after 7 days.

Make.com vs Zapier
Invoice overdue → two-step email reminder sequence
Make result The branching logic (if no response after 3 days, then if still unpaid after 7 days) was handled natively using routers and filters. Clean, visual, and easy to debug. Setup: ~40 minutes. Worked exactly as designed.
Zapier result Multi-step conditional logic required Paths, which is only available on Professional plan (~£44/mo). On Starter, this automation isn't possible as a single Zap. The task count also adds up quickly across a two-email sequence.
Winner Make.com ✓

The Verdict

Make.com wins for most UK solo operators and small teams. The reasons: lower cost ceiling at equivalent capability, better support for complex multi-step and conditional logic, and a visual canvas that — once you understand it — makes debugging significantly faster than Zapier's list view.

Zapier wins on simplicity. The learning curve is lower, the documentation is better, and for basic two-step automations (trigger → action, no branching), you'll get it working faster. If your automations are simple and you're comfortable paying more for a smoother experience, Zapier is a fair choice.

The recommendation: Start with Make.com's free tier (1,000 ops/month). Spend two evenings learning the canvas. By the time you've hit the free limit, you'll know whether you need the Core plan (£8/mo). Zapier's free tier is 100 tasks/month — too limited to evaluate properly.

Learning curve note

Make.com has a steeper initial learning curve than Zapier. The visual scenario builder looks intimidating at first. Give it two full evenings before judging it — the payoff in flexibility and cost is worth the investment.
