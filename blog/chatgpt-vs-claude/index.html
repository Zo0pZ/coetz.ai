AI Battle

ChatGPT vs Claude in 2025:
Which Should You Actually Use?

7 min read Tool Comparison May 2025 No paid placement

Every week someone asks me which AI to use. The honest answer: it depends on the task, and most people are using the wrong one for at least half of what they do. I ran both through five real-world tests — same brief, measured results, no affiliate commission steering the verdict.

Test setup

Models tested: ChatGPT (GPT-4o) and Claude (Sonnet 4.5) — both on their paid plans. Five categories, five briefs. I judged on output quality, instruction-following and edit-time needed. No sponsorships involved in this comparison.

Round 1: Long-form Writing

Brief: Write a 400-word opinion piece on why most people are using AI wrong, aimed at UK professionals. Maintain an authoritative but accessible tone. Don't use bullet points.

ChatGPT vs Claude
Long-form Writing — 400-word opinion piece
ChatGPT result Competent but generic. Strong opener, but fell into predictable structure. Used the phrase "in today's fast-paced world" unprompted (I had to edit it out). Required one round of revision to sharpen the voice.
Claude result Tighter, more distinctive voice from the first draft. Better paragraph variation and a stronger conclusion. Needed almost no editing. Followed the "no bullet points" instruction without a reminder.
Winner Claude ✓

Round 2: Research Synthesis

Brief: Summarise the current state of AI regulation in the UK, covering the main legislation, the government's stance, and what it means for businesses. Keep it under 300 words.

ChatGPT vs Claude
Research — UK AI regulation summary
ChatGPT result More up-to-date references, including recent 2024 developments. Web browsing access (with the right plugin) gave it a clear advantage here. Stayed under the word count on first attempt.
Claude result Strong structural summary but knowledge cutoff showed — missed a couple of recent government statements. Good accuracy on what it did include, but I'd want to verify the specifics before publishing.
Winner ChatGPT ✓

Round 3: Coding

Brief: Write a Python script that reads a CSV of email addresses, removes duplicates, validates format, and outputs a cleaned list. Include comments explaining what each section does.

ChatGPT vs Claude
Coding — Python CSV processing script
ChatGPT result Functional and well-commented. Used regex for email validation correctly. Needed a small fix to handle edge cases (empty rows). Good but required one follow-up prompt to add error handling.
Claude result Produced cleaner code with error handling already included on first pass. Better variable naming. Also added a brief explanation of the validation logic unprompted. Ran correctly without any follow-up.
Winner Claude ✓

Round 4: Creative Brief

Brief: Write 5 Instagram caption options for a post announcing a new AI prompt pack. Each must be under 150 words, have a distinct hook, and end with a call to action. Target audience: UK professionals aged 25–40.

ChatGPT vs Claude
Creative — 5 Instagram caption variants
ChatGPT result Five genuinely distinct options with different emotional hooks — curiosity, urgency, social proof, humour, aspiration. All hit the word count. Two were ready to post with minor tweaks.
Claude result Good options but three of the five had a similar "here's the thing about AI…" structure. Less tonal range than expected. The writing quality within each caption was excellent, but the variety fell short.
Winner ChatGPT ✓

Round 5: Complex Reasoning

Brief: I run a solo content business in the UK. I have £500/month to spend on AI tools. I currently use ChatGPT Plus and Notion. Recommend what to cut, what to add, and why — prioritising ROI.

ChatGPT vs Claude
Reasoning — AI stack recommendation for solo business
ChatGPT result Reasonable recommendations but stayed generic. Didn't push back on any assumptions. Recommended tools that totalled over £500 without flagging the issue. Good starting point, not a useful recommendation without more prompting.
Claude result Asked a clarifying question before answering (what type of content do you make?), then gave a specific stack with UK pricing, explained trade-offs, flagged that two tools overlapped, and came in under £500. Noticeably more useful.
Winner Claude ✓

The Verdict

Use Claude when: you're writing long-form content, need precise instruction-following, doing complex reasoning, or writing code that needs to work first time. Claude's constraint-adherence is noticeably better — it reads and follows the brief rather than approximating it.

Use ChatGPT when: you need current information (its browsing tool is better integrated), you're generating multiple creative variants that need tonal variety, or you need access to the broader plugin ecosystem.

Practically: I use Claude as my daily driver for writing and reasoning, and dip into ChatGPT when I need something recent or when I want to generate a wider range of creative options. Both are worth the ~£15–20/month on a paid plan. The free tiers are genuinely useful for testing.

Important caveat

Both models are updated regularly. This comparison was run in May 2025. The gap between them may narrow or widen with future releases — always test both on your specific use case before deciding.
