Perplexity AI Review 2025:
Worth It Over Google?

5 min read 30-day test May 2025 No affiliate

I used Perplexity as my primary and only search tool for 30 days. No Google fallback. Every query — research, news, technical questions, quick facts — went through Perplexity first. Here's an honest account of where it won, where it lost, and whether the Pro plan justifies £17/month.

Test conditions

30 days, Perplexity Pro plan, tested across five categories. No affiliate commission from Perplexity. I'm in the Founder's AI Stack as a paid tool, which should tell you the verdict before you read it — but the caveats matter too.

What Perplexity Actually Is

Perplexity is an AI-powered search engine. You ask a question in natural language, it searches the web in real time, synthesises what it finds, and returns a structured answer with citations. It's not a chatbot — it doesn't maintain conversation context the way Claude does — but it's dramatically faster than opening ten links and reading them yourself.

The core interface is simple: a single search bar. Results include a synthesised answer at the top, followed by the sources it drew from (clickable), and related questions. The Pro plan unlocks deeper search modes, file uploads, and access to multiple underlying AI models.

Five Use Cases: Scored Honestly

Research
Topic & market research
Dramatically faster than Google for synthesising a topic overview. Cited sources allow quick verification. The Pro "Deep Research" mode is genuinely useful for longer research tasks.
Perplexity wins
Current events
News and recent updates
Strong for recent tool launches, company announcements, and news. The real-time web access makes it significantly better than Claude for anything that happened in the last few months.
Perplexity wins
Technical queries
How-to and technical docs
Good for explaining concepts with citations. Weaker than Google for finding specific documentation pages or Stack Overflow answers — the synthesis sometimes loses the precise code snippet you need.
Draw
Local / UK-specific
UK-specific searches
A notable weakness. "Best plumber in Bristol" or "HMRC self-assessment deadline" sometimes returns US-centric results first. Google's local index is still significantly better for UK-specific queries.
Google wins
Shopping / comparison
Product research
Decent for reading reviews and summarising product categories, but Google Shopping integration and local retailer results make Google the better choice for actual purchase decisions.
Google wins
Academic / deep research
Complex research synthesis
The Pro plan's Deep Research mode produces detailed, multi-source reports with proper citations. For writing articles, market analysis, or due diligence — genuinely impressive.
Perplexity wins

Free vs Pro: Is £17/Month Worth It?

The free plan is useful. You get real-time web search, cited answers, and the standard Perplexity model. For casual research, it's more than enough.

The Pro plan adds: Deep Research mode (longer, more thorough multi-source reports), choice of underlying model (GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet, or Perplexity's own), file upload and analysis, and higher daily search limits.

Who should pay: Anyone doing research as a core part of their work — content creators, consultants, analysts, journalists. The Deep Research mode alone produces work in 5 minutes that would previously take 45. At that productivity multiplier, £17/month is trivially justified.

Who shouldn't bother: Casual users who search a few times a week. The free plan is entirely sufficient. Don't pay just to have Pro — use the free tier until you hit its limits, then decide.

The 30-Day Verdict

Perplexity didn't replace Google — I still use Google for local searches, shopping, and finding specific web pages. But it replaced Google for about 60% of my daily searches: research, "what is X", "how does Y work", "what happened with Z", competitive landscape queries.

That 60% is the high-value work. The time saved on research synthesis has measurably improved my output quality — not because the AI does the thinking, but because I arrive at the thinking stage with much better raw material.

The Pro plan at £17/month: yes, worth it if research is a significant part of your work. No, if you're a casual user. Start free, upgrade when you find yourself frustrated by the limits.

The best Perplexity prompt structure

For best results, be specific: "Summarise the current state of [topic] in the UK, focusing on [specific angle]. Include developments from the last 6 months only." Vague queries get vague answers. Treat it like a research brief, not a search bar.
