Freelance Stack · UK Pricing

The UK Freelancer's
AI Toolkit: Under £30/mo

5 min read Freelance Stack May 2025 GBP pricing

The Founder's AI Stack runs to around £100/month. That's reasonable for a business, but over-engineered for most freelancers starting out. This is the lean version: eight tools covering writing, research, invoicing, client communication, and self-promotion — total cost under £30/month, with four of the eight being completely free.

Who this is for

UK-based freelancers — writers, designers, consultants, developers, marketers — who want to use AI to work better and faster without spending a significant fraction of a small invoice on tools. All GBP pricing, all tested. No paid placements in this list.

The Eight Tools

01
Claude (Free tier)
What it does: Your primary AI for writing, editing, drafting proposals, writing client emails, and complex thinking tasks. The free tier is generous enough for daily freelance use if you're not hammering it constantly. Use the R-C-F prompt framework to get the most out of it — the quality difference between a bad prompt and a good one on the free tier is significant. Upgrade to Pro (£15/mo) when you find yourself hitting limits mid-project.
Free
Pro: £15/mo
02
Notion (Free tier)
What it does: Client tracker, project notes, invoice log, idea capture, and a prompt library all in one place. The free plan's block limit is adequate for a single freelancer. Set up a simple database: one row per client, with columns for status, last contact date, invoiced amount, and notes. Linking this to a prompt library turns Notion into an AI-augmented workflow system. See the full Second Brain setup guide for the complete structure.
Free
Plus: £12/mo
03
Perplexity (Free tier)
What it does: Research. Before a client call, before writing a brief, before quoting on a project you've not done before — Perplexity synthesises what you need to know in minutes. The free tier provides real-time web search with cited answers. Upgrade to Pro (£17/mo) only if research is a significant daily activity. For occasional use, free is enough. See the full Perplexity review for what Pro actually unlocks.
Free
Pro: £17/mo
04
Otter.ai (Free tier)
What it does: Transcribes your client calls automatically. Join a Zoom or Teams call with Otter running, and you get a full transcript plus a summary within minutes of the call ending. Feed the transcript into Claude to extract: action items, client requirements, questions to follow up on. The free plan gives 300 minutes/month of transcription — enough for most freelancers. Practical use: stop taking manual notes during calls. Let Otter capture it, use Claude to process it.
Free
Pro: £8/mo
05
Grammarly (Free tier)
What it does: Grammar, spelling, and clarity checks on everything client-facing. Install the browser extension and it works inside Gmail, Notion, your portfolio site's CMS, anywhere you type. The free tier catches the errors that matter. Set it to British English — this one setting alone prevents the most common Americanisms sneaking into UK professional copy. The paid plan's tone detection is nice but not necessary at this stage.
Free
Pro: ~£10/mo
06
Make.com (Free tier)
What it does: Automates repetitive tasks between your apps. Start with one automation: when a new project row is added to Notion, automatically create a folder in Google Drive with the client name and date. That's it. One automation saves a small but real amount of time every week, and building it teaches you how the platform works. The free tier's 1,000 operations/month is enough for two or three simple automations. See the full Make vs Zapier comparison for what the paid tier unlocks.
Free
Core: £8/mo
07
Canva (Free tier with AI)
What it does: Design tool with built-in AI — Magic Write for copy, Text to Image for visuals, background removal, and presentation templates. For freelancers who need to produce proposals, case studies, or social content without a design background, Canva's free tier AI features are genuinely capable. Best freelance use case: client proposal decks that look professional without costing a designer's day rate. The pro version (£10/mo) unlocks unlimited background removal and brand kit — worth it once you're producing client-facing work regularly.
Free
Pro: £10/mo
08
FreshBooks (Lite — £11/mo)
What it does: Invoicing, expense tracking, and time logging — all HMRC-compatible and built for UK sole traders. Not strictly an "AI tool," but it integrates with AI tools (Make.com can automate reminders and Notion can log payments from it) and it solves the admin problem that eats disproportionate freelance time. The Lite plan handles up to five clients — enough for most freelancers starting out. The alternative is hand-building this in Notion + Google Sheets, which is free but slower.

That's £11/month if you stay on free tiers everywhere except FreshBooks. If you add Claude Pro (£15/mo), you're at £26/month — still under the £30 target, and a stack that handles the majority of what a freelance operation needs day-to-day.

The Priority Order

Don't try to set everything up on day one. Here's the order that makes sense:

  • Week 1: Claude free + Grammarly free. Start using AI for every draft you write. Get used to prompting well.
  • Week 2: Notion free. Build the client tracker and prompt library.
  • Week 3: Otter.ai free. Run it on your next three client calls. See what you'd have missed without the transcript.
  • Month 2: Add Perplexity free and Make.com free. One automation. One research tool.
  • When you're billing consistently: FreshBooks Lite (£11/mo). This is the one that actually saves you tax headaches.
The honest truth

The tools are not the hard part. The habit is the hard part. Using Claude for every client email for two weeks is more valuable than setting up a perfect ten-tool stack and using none of them consistently. Start with Claude. Everything else is additive.
